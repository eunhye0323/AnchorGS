AnchorGS:
Real-View-Guided Depth Refinement for Sparse-View Gaussian Splatting with a Generative Prior

M.S. Thesis Research

Eunhye Choi1
1School of Computing, KAIST  

Abstract

Generative sparse-view reconstruction uses synthesized views to supplement limited observations, but inaccurate geometric targets can compromise surface reconstruction. In G4Splat, plane refinement combines depth estimates from real and generated views, allowing errors in plane estimation and grouping to propagate through depth supervision and Gaussian initialization. As generated-view depths depend on earlier reconstructions, these errors can persist across successive reconstruction rounds.

We propose AnchorGS, which uses the raw chart depths of the real input views as a persistent geometric reference across all reconstruction rounds. For real views, a scale-invariant log-depth loss with a constant weight replaces G4Splat's decaying direct depth loss. For generated views, each plane group observed by the real views is refitted to real-view chart points only, and the generated views' depth targets are traced onto these planes by ray–plane intersection; the same traced depths initialize the Gaussians of groups that pass a plane-fit residual check. The targets are rebuilt in every round, so each round is supervised against real-view geometry rather than against earlier reconstructions.

Method

AnchorGS builds on G4Splat's pipeline of Gaussian optimization and view generation.

1. Persistent real-view depth supervision

G4Splat progressively reduces the weight of its depth loss on real views. AnchorGS instead applies a constant-weight, scale-invariant log-depth loss to the chart depth before plane refinement:

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{real}} = \frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{SI}}}{2}\, \operatorname{Var}_{u}\!\left[ \log \hat D(u) - \log D_{\mathrm{raw}}(u) \right], \qquad \lambda_{\mathrm{SI}} = 20. \]

Here, \(\hat D\) is the rendered depth and \(D_{\mathrm{raw}}\) is the raw chart depth. This loss maintains supervision on within-view depth structure throughout training while allowing a per-view multiplicative scale difference. Raw chart depth also provides the depth-order target.

2. Real-anchored depth targets for generated views

G4Splat fits global planes using points from both real and generated views. We retain its plane groups but refit eligible planes using only real-view chart points, preventing generated-view depth errors from directly entering these fits.

For a fitted plane with unit normal \(\mathbf{n}_g\) and a point \(\mathbf{c}_g\), the target depth in generated view \(j\) is obtained by ray–plane intersection:

\[ D^{\mathrm{anchor}}_{j}(u) = \frac{\mathbf{n}_g^{\top}\left(\mathbf{c}_g - \mathbf{C}_j\right)}{\mathbf{n}_g^{\top}\, \mathbf{r}_j(u)}, \]

where \(\mathbf{C}_j\) is the camera center and \(\mathbf{r}_j(u)\) is the pixel ray, parameterized by camera depth.

These targets are used in two ways:

  • Depth supervision. Valid intersections replace the original depth targets within eligible plane masks.
  • Gaussian initialization.
  • Gaussian initialization. Anchored depths are used only for groups that pass a robust plane-fitting residual check.

Other pixels retain G4Splat's depth targets. Generated-view normal and curvature priors and depth-order targets remain unchanged. Anchored targets are recomputed before each round that includes generated views, using the current plane groups and the fixed real-view chart depths.
